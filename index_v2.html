▸ INFLATION — THEORY PRIMER

Cosmic Inflation

Inflation is a period of accelerated expansion in the very early universe (t~10⁻³⁶ s), driven by the potential energy V(φ) of a scalar field φ (the inflaton). It solves the horizon, flatness, and monopole problems and generates the primordial density perturbations observed in the CMB.

Slow-Roll Parameters

ε(φ) = ½(V′/V)² — measures the slope of V. Inflation occurs while ε < 1 and ends when ε = 1.

η(φ) = V″/V — measures the curvature of V. The slow-roll approximation requires ε, |η| ≪ 1.

CMB Observables

nₛ = 1 − 6ε★ + 2η★ is the scalar spectral index (tilt of the power spectrum). Planck 2018: nₛ = 0.965 ± 0.004.

r = 16ε★ is the tensor-to-scalar ratio (gravitational waves). Current bound: r < 0.064 (Planck+BICEP/Keck).

E-folds & Pivot Scale

The number of e-folds is N(φ) = ∫ V/V′ dφ (in reduced Planck units). CMB observables are evaluated at the pivot field value φ★ corresponding to N★ ≈ 50–60 e-folds before inflation ends, depending on the reheating history.

Mukhanov–Sasaki Equation

The exact quantum mode equation for scalar perturbations is u_k″ + (k² − z″/z) u_k = 0, where z = aφ̇/H = a dφ/dN is the pump field and primes denote conformal-time derivatives.

Starting in the adiabatic (WKB) vacuum u_k = 1/√(2ω), ω² = k² − z″/z, deep inside the horizon, each mode oscillates then freezes out after horizon crossing (k = aH), seeding the classical perturbation ℛ_k = u_k/z.

The Pump Field, Exactly

With ε₁ = ½(dφ/dN)² and ε₂ = d ln ε₁/dN, writing L = ln z gives the exact identity

z″/z = (aH)²[ (1−ε₁)L′ + L″ + L′² ], L′ = 1 + ε₂/2

No slow-roll expansion is used. To first order this reduces to 2 + 5ε_V − 3η_V — note the coefficient of η_V: expansions written in the Hubble parameters (2 + 2ε − 3η_H, η_H = η_V − ε_V) must not be mixed with potential ones. Tensors use a″/a = (aH)²(2 − ε₁), also exact.

Exact Background

The background is not the slow-roll approximation but the full inflaton equation in e-folds,

d²φ/dN² + (3 − ε₁)(dφ/dN + V_φ/V) = 0, H² = V/(3 − ε₁)

integrated forward in time from the slow-roll attractor until ε₁ = 1, which defines the end of inflation self-consistently. N★ is then measured back from that endpoint.

MS vs Slow-Roll Accuracy

The slow-roll result 𝒫ℛ = H²/(8π²ε) evaluated at horizon exit is accurate to ~1% for standard single-field models with ε, |η| ≪ 1. Deviations appear near features in V(φ) (bumps, steps, inflection points) or when slow-roll is violated — exactly the physics the Relative Error and Running nₛ(k) plots diagnose.

Because 𝒫ℛ and 𝒫T both scale linearly with V, the CMB normalisation is a single number applied to both spectra — so r = 𝒫T/𝒫ℛ and the consistency relation r = −8n_t are genuine numerical predictions, not inputs.

Primordial Black Holes

A mode re-entering the horizon in radiation domination carries 𝒫δ = (16/81)(kR)⁴T²(kR)𝒫ℛ. Smoothing with a Gaussian window gives the variance σ²(M), and Press–Schechter gives the collapse fraction β(M) = erfc(δ_c/√2σ) with δ_c ≈ 0.45.

Redshifting ρ_PBH/s = const to today gives f_PBH = Ω_PBH/Ω_DM. Because β is a complementary error function, f_PBH is exponentially sensitive to σ, hence to δ_c and to the peak amplitude of 𝒫ℛ: reaching f ~ 1 requires 𝒫ℛ ~ 10⁻², seven orders of magnitude above the CMB value.

Scalar-Induced Gravitational Waves

At second order in perturbation theory the same large 𝒫ℛ that makes PBHs unavoidably sources tensor modes. In radiation domination the oscillation-averaged result is a convolution of 𝒫ℛ with itself against the kernel Ī²(u,v).

Since both the PBH mass and the GW frequency are fixed by the same horizon scale at re-entry, f_GW ∝ M_PBH^(−1/2): asteroid-mass PBHs (10¹⁷–10²³ g, the open dark-matter window) predict a mHz signal in the LISA/DECIGO band, while solar-mass PBHs predict a nHz signal in the PTA band. This makes the scenario falsifiable.
